# **E-Commerce Customer Segmentation: From Unsupervised K-Means to Supervised SHAP Interpretation**

## **1. Project Overview & Business Objective**
Standard customer segmentation often relies on unsupervised distance-based algorithms (like K-Means) combined with traditional RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) frameworks. While mathematically sound, these "Black Box" models fail to explain *why* a customer belongs to a specific group, making it difficult to formulate actionable marketing strategies.

**The Objective:** To bridge the gap between mathematical proximity and behavioral interpretability. 
We treated this as a **Supervised Surrogate Learning** task. By taking the K-Means cluster labels generated by Group 1 on the Brazilian E-Commerce dataset (Olist) and training a highly optimized Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) to predict those labels, we successfully reverse-engineered the clustering logic. Using **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)**, we extracted the true causal drivers of the segments, refuting naive assumptions and providing proactive business strategies.

---

## **2. Dataset & Feature Engineering**

The base dataset consisted of **93,358 unique customers** sourced from the Brazilian E-Commerce platform, Olist. To ensure the supervised surrogate model captured a comprehensive behavioral profile, we executed a rigorous feature engineering pipeline to transition from raw transactional data to a **13-Feature Composite Matrix**.

### **2.1. Feature Construction (The Two Tiers)**

**Tier 1: The Baseline (Reconstruction)**
To ensure our supervised model could accurately learn the ground truth established by the K-Means clustering (Group 1), we retained the **7 original features** that mathematically defined those clusters:
*   `recency_days`, `frequency`, `monetary` (The standard RFM framework).
*   `avg_review_score` (Proxy for customer satisfaction).
*   `avg_installments` (Proxy for credit utilization).
*   `avg_freight` (Shipping cost friction).
*   `avg_items_per_order` (Basket size/complexity).

**Tier 2: The Augmented Features (Hypothesis Testing)**
We engineered **3 net-new features** from the raw Olist datasets (`olist_orders`, `olist_payments`, `olist_customers`) to test specific business hypotheses and provide the model with deeper contextual signals:
1.  **`credit_card_usage_ratio` (Financial):** We one-hot encoded payment types at the order level and aggregated them per customer. *Hypothesis:* High-value "Champions" prefer the leverage of credit cards over cash/Boleto.
2.  **`avg_delivery_days` (Logistics):** We calculated the exact time-to-deliver by subtracting `order_purchase_timestamp` from `order_delivered_customer_date`. Missing values were imputed using the global median to avoid skewing. *Hypothesis:* Long wait times are the primary driver pushing customers into the "Unhappy" cluster.
3.  **`state_freq_encoding` (Geographic Context):** We mapped customer IDs to their geographic state. To avoid exploding the feature space with 27 highly sparse One-Hot columns, we utilized **Frequency Encoding** (replacing the state name with its prevalence ratio, e.g., SP = 0.42, RR = 0.01). *Hypothesis:* Remote states experience distinct logistical friction compared to major hubs.

### **2.2. Preprocessing & Data Quality Check**
Before modeling, the engineered dataset (`engineered_data_full.csv`) underwent a final audit:
*   **Null Handling:** All nulls in the Baseline features were filled using the dataset mean, matching the logic of the original clustering group. Total nulls remaining across all 13 columns: **0**.
*   **Transformation:** The continuous numerical features (Monetary, Recency, etc.) were processed through a pipeline applying the **Yeo-Johnson Power Transformation** (to handle severe right-skew) followed by **StandardScaler** (to normalize variance for distance-based comparisons).

### **2.3. The Stratification Mandate (Splitting Strategy)**

An analysis of the Target Variable (`cluster_id`) revealed a **severe class imbalance**:
*   Cluster 0 (Thrifty/Standard): **49.0%**
*   Cluster 1 (Dormant Credit Users): **38.6%**
*   Cluster 3 (Unhappy/Logistics Risk): **9.4%**
*   **Cluster 2 (The VIP Champions): Only 3.0%**

$\Rightarrow$ **The Risk of Leakage:** A standard random split posed a critical statistical danger. A random 15% Test set could easily under-sample or entirely miss the minority "VIP" class (Cluster 2), rendering hyperparameter tuning unstable and evaluation metrics artificially inflated (The "Accuracy Trap").

$\Rightarrow$ **The Solution:** We enforced strict **Stratified Partitioning** based on the Target Variable:
*   **Training Set (70%):** 65,350 rows. The Learning Environment.
*   **Validation Set (15%):** 14,004 rows. The Tuning Environment (used exclusively by Optuna).
*   **Test Set (15%):** 14,004 rows. The Final Evaluation Environment (used exclusively for SHAP).

The variance in class distribution between the Train and Test sets was validated to be a statistically negligible **0.00002**, guaranteeing that our models were trained and tested on mathematically identical representations of the customer base.

## **4. Model Selection & Hyperparameter Tuning**
We evaluated 8 distinct algorithms (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, ExtraTrees, KNN, MLP) to establish a baseline. Evaluation relied strictly on **Macro F1** (to ensure fairness to the 3% minority class) and **Log Loss** (to measure probability confidence).

**Bayesian Optimization (Optuna):**
The top 3 tree-based models were subjected to rigorous hyperparameter tuning to minimize Log Loss ("Beat the Default" heuristic). 

**Final Tuned Leaderboard (Top 3):**
| Rank | Model | Macro F1 | Log Loss | Confidence Lift (vs. Default) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **LightGBM (Champion)** | **0.9980** | **0.0080** | **+17.1%** |
| 2 | XGBoost | 0.9981 | 0.0084 | +8.6% |
| 3 | CatBoost | 0.9984 | 0.0088 | +34.6% |

$\Rightarrow$ **Implication:** LightGBM achieved the "Holy Trinity" of Machine Learning: Highest Confidence (lowest error), Perfect Stability, and High Computational Efficiency. The 99.8% accuracy proves the clusters are deterministic, validating the surrogate approach.

## **5. Interpretability: SHAP Insights & The Lean Model**

Using `shap.TreeExplainer` on the Test Set, we extracted the directional impact of every feature.

### **Key Business Discoveries:**
1.  **The "Hidden Driver":** `avg_installments` is the absolute **#1 Global Driver** (SHAP Impact: 7.89), outweighing `monetary` (5.18). Financial leverage (how they pay) segregates the market more cleanly than purchase history (what they bought).
2.  **Symptom vs. Root Cause (Cluster 3):** While Cluster 3 has terrible reviews, SHAP revealed that `avg_items_per_order` is the true defining driver. Bulk buying leads to fulfillment friction, which *causes* the bad review. 
3.  **The Definition of Loyalty (Cluster 2):** `frequency` is the exclusive positive driver for VIPs, distinguishing them mathematically from the debt-burdened "One-Hit Wonders" in Cluster 1.

### **The "Lean Model" Experiment:**
We performed a Recursive Feature Addition loop to find the point of diminishing returns. 
*   **Result:** We achieved **99.25% Accuracy** utilizing only **5 features** (`avg_installments`, `monetary`, `avg_items_per_order`, `avg_freight`, `frequency`).
*   **Feature Reduction:** 50% of the data (including `avg_review_score` and `avg_delivery_days`) is mathematically redundant.

## **6. Strategic Conclusion: Comparative Analysis**

By contrasting our Supervised approach with standard Unsupervised methodologies, we delivered three major business upgrades:

| Dimension | Standard RFM / Centroid View | Supervised SHAP View | Business Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Driver Identification** | Focused on Transaction Volume (Monetary). | Focused on Financial Behavior (Installments). | Shifted strategy from discounting to financing. |
| **Risk Mitigation** | Wait for a 1-star review to identify unhappy users (Reactive). | Identifies High-Risk users via bulk item counts in cart (Proactive). | Enables real-time logistics intervention. |
| **Data Efficiency** | Requires post-purchase lagging indicators (Delivery times, Reviews). | Relies solely on leading indicators available at Checkout. | **Transforms reporting tool into a Real-Time Prediction Engine.** |
